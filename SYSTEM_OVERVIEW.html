1The big picture

The system replaces the operational parts of Odoo. It is not an HR system: no payroll, leave balances, recruitment or performance reviews. Its job is to answer, for every day: which flights are there, which team handles each one, who should be at work, who actually came, and how much overtime they did.

Attendance is the core. To know whether someone is late, missing or doing overtime, the system must first know when they should be working, which is what the shift cycles are for.

2Who uses it

People sign in with their ID number and a 6-digit code that an admin issues. There are no passwords. A code expires (7 days by default), five wrong tries lock the account, and an admin can issue a new code at any time.

Supervisors are staff members listed on each team. For now they cannot sign in or record anything; they get a different job later. They do appear in the roster, on their own personal shift cycle.

Permissions are checked on the server for every page and every save. Hiding a button is only cosmetic: an operations manager who sends an attendance request anyway gets 403 Forbidden.

4Shifts and cycles Next

Only two shifts, both 12 hours

The admin enters the Day start and Night start once, for the whole airport. Each shift ends 12 hours after it starts. A night shift ends the next morning but belongs to the date it starts. The times below are an example; the admin sets the real ones.

A cycle is a repeating list of days

Each team gets a cycle of 1 to 28 days. Every day in it is D (Day), N (Night) or O (Off). The admin paints it cell by cell and picks a start date (cycle day 1). From then on it repeats forever, so there is nothing to regenerate each month.

Try it: click a cell to switch it between D, N and O, change the length, or start from one of the examples. The calendar shows the next four weeks.

Changes never rewrite the past

When the admin changes a cycle, they choose the date it takes effect. The old version stays for every day before it, so last month's roster and reports look exactly as they did. The same goes for shift times: each cycle keeps a copy of the times it was saved with. The admin's changes go live directly; there is no approval step.

5Whose shift is it? Next

The roster is calculated when someone looks at it, never stored. For one person on one date, the system checks these in order and takes the first that applies:

1. Absencesick, leave, no-show or other: they are not expected
2. Overridea one-off change for that date: extra shift, day off, or a swap
3. Personal cycleadmin staff and supervisors follow their own cycle instead of the team's
4. Team cyclethe cycle of the team they belong to on that date

Because everything is calculated, the admin's editor shows a live four-week preview for every team member as they paint the cycle, with no waiting.

6Team moves, overrides and swaps Next

Moving someone to another team

The admin picks the date the move takes effect: today or later, never in the past. Before that date the person follows the old team's cycle; from it, the new team's. A future move shows on the employee ("→ Team B from 3 Nov").

Overrides

For a single date the admin can give someone a Day shift, a Night shift, or a day Off, with a reason. A Day or Night shift on a day they were meant to be off is an extra shift, and all of it counts as overtime.

Swaps

Two people swapping shifts is one action: both sides are saved together, and cancelling the swap cancels both. A swapped shift is a normal shift, not overtime, because each person gives up a day in exchange.

7Attendance Next

The admin opens Attendance, picks a date and a team, and sees everyone rostered on each shift with one-tap buttons: check-in, check-out, break start and break end. The page refreshes itself so the status stays current.

Rules

  • Breaks are paid. Worked time is check-in to check-out; breaks are not subtracted.
  • A check-in or check-out belongs to a shift if it happens between 2 h before it starts and 7 h after it ends. A night shift's 07:05 check-out counts for the previous evening's shift.
  • Records are never edited. A mistake is fixed by adding a correction with a reason; the original stays visible.
  • Under 12 hours on a rostered shift is flagged short.

8Overtime Next

Overtime is counted in hours only (no money), per day, with no legal rules and nothing carried over from one day to the next.

Normal rostered shift

Overtime = time worked past 12 h.
12 h 40 m worked → 40 m.

Extra shift on an off day

All of it is overtime.
A full 12 h extra shift → 12 h.

Rounding

To the nearest 15 min.
7 m → 0 · 8 m → 15 m · 40 m → 45 m

Two categories

Day or Night, by the shift it belongs to. Shown as 2.25 h and 2:15.

A warning (never a block) appears when someone works more than 18 h on one shift date.

Only an admin approves overtime. The approval stores the hours approved. If a correction later changes that day's hours, it shows "changed since approval" so nothing slips through.

9Reports Next

The daily attendance report shows, per team and shift: how many were scheduled, present, late, missing, absent, off and unscheduled; and per person, hours worked and overtime (Day or Night, approved or pending). It prints cleanly in English and Arabic.

The totals always match the underlying records, and a past day's report never changes when a cycle, shift time or team is changed afterwards.

Flights tie in too: when a team is assigned to a flight, the system will warn if the handling window falls on that team's day off or outside its shift.

10Rules that always hold

Server-checked permissions

Every page and save checks the user's permission on the server.

Everything audited

Each change writes an audit record in the same step, with the reason when one is needed.

Nothing deleted

Flights are cancelled, people and teams deactivated, records corrected, never erased.

No double-booking

The database itself rejects a team on two overlapping flights.

Damascus time

Stored as exact instants, always shown and entered in Damascus time.

English & Arabic

Every screen in both languages; times and flight numbers stay left-to-right.
